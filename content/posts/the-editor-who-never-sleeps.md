The youth is well-renowned as a sponge, to our success or demise. Knowledge, morals & idealogies are fed to us by gleeful parents until we choke. Then they set us out into the world hoping their words can forever shield us. and they do for a time. We scoff at others' words silently in our minds, thinking them fools for their ways, that only what we have been taught is correct on all fronts. We hastily turn away from opposing opinions, thoughts & actions thinking it makes us strong in our personal beliefs but it only leads to a weak wall of identity.

Our defences are impenetrable we think. We will never succumb to others, we say. But then we grow. We meet a myriad of new people, get exposed to thousands and thousands of personalities online. And slowly our defences crumble bit by bit. Not truly going away no but suddenly you have lost your foundations for a day or two. They come back but disappear once again and thus a cycle begins. You have to then adjust to the feeling of losing oneself.

Asking for help is not a weakness, it is a sign of self-awareness of personal limitations and willingness to grow, but we cannot confuse it with asking others to form our identity
There is a risk in entirely molding yourself to those you put on a pedestal only to find out the veins of your heart and blood of your body and tissue of your brain are completely different.

The modern age is determined to fit us into these boxes of aesthetics to make our existence feel worthwhile. Your style, interests and looks must all be tied by a colour scheme and lifestyle otherwise you have failed as a person. It is an easy trap to fall into in search of self-fulfillment and societal approval but confining ourselves to any one aesthetic will never give us identity and contentment because human beings are not meant to be rigid lines of conformity. The things that we love and the people that we are are the only things that can and will define us. Not packaged capitalism disguised as identity.

We learn to climb a ladder out of the pits of self-loathing and pity, but simultaneously having to resist the temptation of the gilded cage of arrogance and self-righteousness.
At these precarious adolescent ages we are suspended between the innocence of our childhood and the seemingly bleak reality of adulthood constantly hearing whispers from those above us about our path and personality, receiving warnings of foregoing personal passions in exchange for stability.

We are crushed beneath those iron heavy deterrants, heavy breaths on our necks.
But when you truly feel those words inside and out you realise they are often laced with disproportionate fear disguised as selfless care. The world evolves as generations dissolve and it is up to each group to decide the risks and rewards for themselves.

There are no answers at this age and I cannot offer them. What I can offer is urging us all to learn to think for ourselves. To let our true interests and loves be guided by our inner child and not what society insists for us. To surround ourselves with shining, bright people that encourage us to build our own light and not dim ourselves or others in the process. This life we have only belongs to us and it is up to us to decide who we are at the end of it.
